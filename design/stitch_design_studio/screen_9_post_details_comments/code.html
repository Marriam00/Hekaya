Revisiting Calvino's Invisible Cities tonight. There is a line that struck me with renewed force:

format_quote

“With cities, it is as with dreams: everything imaginable can be dreamed, but even the most unexpected dream is a rebus that conceals a desire, or its reverse, a fear.”

— Page 44, City of Zaira

How many of us read a book once for the story, and the second time for the city it builds inside our thoughts? When I first read this copy borrowed from Karim last winter, I saw it purely as architectural poetry. Reading it now during October rains, it feels like an elegy for forgotten spaces.
